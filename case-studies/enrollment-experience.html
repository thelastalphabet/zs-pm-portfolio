EdTech Self-Service Platform Leavitt School of Health, WGU

Transforming WGU's Enrollment Experience

Rebuilding the self-service enrollment journey for WGU's most complex school — nine programs, healthcare licensing requirements, and 23,000+ learners — earning the WGU EdTech Award of the Year.

01Challenge & Vision

WGU's enrollment process faced critical scalability constraints. Prospects navigated extensive applications requesting unnecessary information, creating friction at every step. Students lacked transparency about timelines and next steps, driving support calls higher.

The core challenge: Enrollment Counselor headcount had to grow proportionally with applicants — an unsustainable model that prevented proactive outreach to the students who needed support most.

North Star: Personalized self-service — build applicant confidence in their program fit, deliver smart intervention for those who need guidance, and accelerate students into their program of choice as quickly as possible.

We measured success on two horizons: Application Complete Rate as a leading indicator of self-service efficiency, and conversion rate as the lagging indicator of long-term enrollment success — targeting a 15% improvement in Enrollment Counselor labor cost, or roughly $6M in savings.

02Understanding the Complexity

The Leavitt School of Health was our most complex transformation yet: 9 diverse programs, with 3,000+ monthly applicants in Prelicensure alone.

Scale & ambition

23,000+ adults becoming nurses and health professionals today, with plans to double enrollment within 3–5 years.

Diverse programs

Nine distinct programs requiring tailored application workflows, from prelicensure to advanced practitioner tracks.

High-stakes requirements

Complex prerequisites — essays, letters of recommendation, RN license validation via Nursys, and background checks via Complio.

Manual bottlenecks

Staff manually created Salesforce objects, managed document workflows in OnBase, and handled unique per-program timing requirements.

03Discovery & Targeted Improvements

I started by building a comprehensive understanding of all nine programs, then interviewed enrollment counselors and admissions staff to uncover pain points. Data analysis revealed completion times and bottlenecks, which we mapped into current-state process flows — the foundation for identifying improvements, documenting new workflows, and securing stakeholder buy-in through a clear change-management plan.

  • A
    Resume upload redesignAdded preview functionality and file-type restrictions (PDF/DOC only) to eliminate incorrect uploads — no more pet photos instead of resumes.
  • B
    Application essay transformationReplaced document upload with a guided form, embedding requirements directly into the application to reduce disqualifications.
  • C
    Recommendation lettersModernized a complicated OnBase workflow to reduce errors and streamline submission and review.

04Flagship Project: RN License Validation, Redesigned

The Challenge2,500+ monthly license validations, with 33% requiring manual review. The decade-old OnBase solution processed licenses but had severe limitations — only 25% of flagged items had an actual issue, wasting staff time. The breaking point: legacy workflows required Banner IDs unavailable in our new self-service system.
The SolutionBuilt a direct SSE Orchestrator-to-Nursys integration, relegating OnBase to document archival only. Added self-service resubmission for applicants with data errors, reduced repetitive PII entry, implemented smart error notifications, and automated transfer-evaluation object creation in Salesforce.

05Winning Stakeholder Buy-In

Business stakeholders initially opposed the redesign given its complexity, multiple license types, and countless edge cases across health programs. I moved them from resistance to approval in six steps:

  • 1
    Acknowledge resistanceNamed the complexity concerns directly instead of arguing past them.
  • 2
    Map every scenarioDocumented each license type, use case, and process flow — old versus new — to demonstrate comprehensive understanding.
  • 3
    Quantify the impactCalculated a 60% total manual-work reduction potential, phasing implementation to deliver a 33% improvement in the MVP.
  • 4
    Communicate benefitsShowcased security improvements, eliminated redundant data entry, and learner self-correction of errors.
  • 5
    Mitigate riskCommitted to extensive UAT and business approval before production deployment — no shortcuts on quality.
  • 6
    Enable changeDocumented comprehensive training and change-management procedures for staff transition.

Automation replaced manual work elsewhere in the flow, too:

BeforeStaff manually created program-profile objects in Salesforce after learners submitted clinical location forms. The form wasn't dynamic — all states and districts appeared regardless of program availability, causing extensive rework.
AfterAdded intelligent form logic showing only available states and filtering districts by selection. Automated Salesforce object creation as students progressed — staff intervention eliminated entirely.
