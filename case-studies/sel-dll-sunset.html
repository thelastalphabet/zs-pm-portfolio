Social Emotional Learning Product & a Strategic Sunset

01About the SEL Product

I led the development of ACT's CASEL-aligned Social Emotional Learning product — a comprehensive solution of curriculum and assessments that helps educators assess and improve their students' social emotional skills. I partnered directly with a team of psychometricians to design and validate assessments for PreK-12 and English Language Learners, including a scoring model validated against a national sample of 5,000+ students, and introduced quality-control safeguards to protect assessment integrity at scale.

02SEL Dashboard: Why & What

Why we built it

School administrators and educators wanted to quickly assess students' SEL skills, but existing PDF reports were slow and not interactive. Competitors had interactive dashboards, and we had already lost several RFPs that required one. Over 80% of our customers were assessment users, so this was a direct response to the voice of the customer — and a lever for market share.

What we built

Hierarchical, role-based access for admins and educators to view and filter students' SEL skills, with a summary of top and lowest-performing skills, quick indicators for flagged or in-progress students, and drill-down by permission level — plus printing, easy navigation, and performance as first-class considerations.

Rollout followed a deliberate sequence: define the MVP and plan future rollouts, run usability testing, define requirements, build a prototype, and release the MVP.

03Dashboard: Challenges & Outcomes

ChallengesCompeting with other priorities across the organization's portfolio delayed the release, and technical limitations capped how much filtering we could add.
OutcomesSales increased 10%, support costs dropped significantly, and customers gave positive feedback on how quickly they could summarize data and take next steps.

04The DLL Sunset

Why sunset it

The product was not actively selling, had no active development, and carried declining revenue against rising maintenance cost — and the organization was no longer focused on the K-8 market it served.

How I built the case

Market research on the competitive landscape, an internal/external cost-and-revenue analysis, an assessment of legacy technology and vendor dependency, a review of usage trends over the years, and direct voice-of-customer input.

Outcome: the cost of supporting the product outweighed its value — remaining competitive would have required significant new investment in technology that wasn't sustainable.
ChallengeA proposed 1+ year sunset timeline wasn't feasible against the business case.
Outcome$600K+ in immediate cost savings, plus $80K+ from selling resource metadata to interested customers.

Execution ran through internal leadership approval, a review of customer and vendor contracts, infrastructure sunset planning, a defined timeline, alternate options for affected customers, and coordinated internal and external communications. I turned this into a reusable sunset framework later adopted company-wide.
