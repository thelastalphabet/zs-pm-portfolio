Conversational AI Assistants: A Product Case Study

Transforming student support through conversational AI — from concept to deployment. Directing WGU's first LLM/RAG-based chatbot as a digital concierge for the entire student journey.

81.7%Accuracy, up from 15%
97.5%Relevance, up from 86%
21%Support call volume reduction
$1M+Annual operational savings
On this page The Challenge Discovery Vision & Tenets Architecture & Evaluation Results Challenges & Solutions Roadmap & Takeaways

01The Challenge

Students frequently got blocked by basic questions when support was unavailable, while support staff spent significant time answering repetitive inquiries — resulting in delayed progress for students and reduced capacity for personalized support.

70K

Monthly support inquiries

Roughly 1 inquiry for every 2 learners at WGU.

60K

Monthly applications

Processed by Admissions and Enrollment teams.

40%

"Night owl" learners

Active between 9pm–1am, when staff aren't available.

02Discovery

UX research findings

Chatbot ranked top 3 preferred channels alongside email and SMS. Students wanted AI as an additional resource, but needed human connection for complex issues — and were frustrated by the lack of 24×7 support.

Market context

42% of higher-ed leaders prioritize AI for customer service (Grammarly, 2025), and chatbots are evolving beyond Q&A into agentic capabilities.

Data analysis

10% of enrollment calls involve questions already on the website. 21% of support inquiries are answered in the Student Handbook. 25–48% of assessment calls are simple how-to requests.

Domain expert input

Reduce cost per new student start, proactively detect and support struggling applicants, and enable real-time chat for instant answers.

03Vision & Product Tenets

Product vision: A digital concierge — a conversational AI companion designed as a trusted guide throughout the entire student journey, from enrollment through graduation — delivering instant support, anticipating the next step, and seamlessly connecting to human experts when it matters most.

I anchored the build in six product tenets so the team could make fast, consistent trade-off calls:

  • 01
    Human Connection FirstHelp learners get information quickly, but never replace the essential human connection in learning.
  • 02
    Do the Heavy LiftingDo for learners what they shouldn't have to do for themselves.
  • 03
    Context-Aware ExperiencesTailor experiences to the learner's context and sentiment so they can choose their next best step.
  • 04
    Safeguards & IntegrityBuild guardrails so support and academic-integrity policies are upheld.
  • 05
    Security & PrivacyUphold the highest standards of security and confidentiality in every interaction.
  • 06
    Continuous ImprovementGet better with learners through ongoing feedback and measurable outcomes.

04Architecture & Evaluation Strategy

Databricks Model Platform

Custom LLM hosted and trained on WGU content.

Genesys Web Messenger

Front-end chat experience and omnichannel platform.

Salesforce CRM

Transcript storage and counselor workspace.

POCs validated integrations before the build phase; the architecture was approved despite known Genesys limitations, given WGU's existing omnichannel investment. Phase 1 was scoped to enrollment content only — website data and public Student Handbook pages, with blog content excluded to maintain focus, refreshed via a daily scrape-chunk-embed pipeline.

Evaluation strategy, in four moves:

  • 1
    Manual evaluation guideBuilt a rubric for SMEs — pass/fail criteria, criticality ratings (low/medium/high), and documentation of glaring issues.
  • 2
    SME testing roundsPulled subject matter experts into accuracy and relevance evaluation as soon as the model was created.
  • 3
    Golden dataset creationUsed SME responses to build an evaluation dataset that evolved into the long-term QA benchmark.
  • 4
    Iterative refinementSimplified Phase 2 to pass/fail, documenting only failures per rubric category.

05Results: Dramatic Improvement

Within three months, the team completed critical foundational development: a model trained on all public WGU content, built-in guardrails, and source-backed responses. Multi-phase evaluation drove significant improvement through AI engineering, product/UX, and enrollment SME testing.

15% → 81.7%

Accuracy

Driven by rubric-based SME testing and a growing golden dataset.

86% → 97.5%

Relevance

Iterative refinement against real student questions.

06Challenges & Solutions

Challenge25+ team members across data engineering, ML, product, UX, and infrastructure — with unclear roles, since this was WGU's first LLM product.
SolutionBuilt a comprehensive roles & responsibilities matrix spanning development, deployment, and production.
ChallengeScope disagreement: the business wanted full website coverage vs. product's FAQ-only recommendation.
SolutionDocumented the risks — scraping time, evaluation complexity, hallucination — and proceeded with the business decision.
ChallengeBusiness insisted on live human hand-off and full Salesforce transcript integration vs. a simple contact-us redirect.
SolutionCalled out the technical complexity and timeline risk, then proceeded with the business priority.

Genesys platform limitations, surfaced during build:

  • !
    UX gapsNo loading indicator for delayed responses (3+ seconds), no streaming for partial responses, no placement for an "AI can make mistakes" warning, and no in-chat form support unlike Salesforce chat.
  • !
    PII maskingApplied universally rather than per-channel; Legal approved proceeding without masking for the authenticated enrollment experience.
  • !
    ResourcingOnly two engineers and one architect on the build — I recommended engaging Genesys for roadmap acceleration, evaluating alternative front-ends while keeping the Genesys backend, and planning for additional engineering capacity.

07Roadmap & Key Takeaways

1–2

MVP Chatbots → Simple Automations

Launch for enrollment and support with human-in-the-loop, then extend to tickets, scheduling, and retrieval agents.

3–4

Proactive Nudges → Advanced Use Cases

Chatbot-driven engagement, then tutoring and motivational support.

5

Agentic Capabilities

From simple workflows to complex, multi-step agentic workflows.

Building WGU's first LLM chatbot required balancing innovation with pragmatism, technical excellence with business urgency, and AI capability with human touch. Clear roles for a 25+ person team, rigorous evaluation that moved accuracy from 15% to 81.7% in three months, disciplined risk documentation, and strong product tenets carried the launch.
